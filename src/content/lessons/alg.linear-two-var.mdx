## A line is a collection of solutions

An equation such as <M t="y=2x+3" /> has many solutions. Each solution is an ordered pair <M t="(x,y)" /> that makes the equation true. In slope-intercept form, <M t="m" /> is the slope and <M t="b" /> is the y-intercept.

## Find and check a point

To find a point, choose an x-value and substitute it into the equation. To check a proposed point, substitute both coordinates and see whether the two sides match. A standard-form equation <M t="Ax+By=C" /> can be rearranged to make <M t="y" /> the subject when <M t="B\ne0" />.

## Common traps

- Writing a point as <M t="(y,x)" /> instead of <M t="(x,y)" />.
- Reading the y-intercept as the x-intercept.
- Assuming one line equation has only one solution pair.

## Desmos steps

1. Enter the equation, such as `y=2x+3`; Desmos draws every solution pair.
2. Add `x=4` to see where that input meets the line.
3. The intersection's y-coordinate is the matching output. Click a point on the line to inspect its coordinates.

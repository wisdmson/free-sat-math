## Ask how the study was done

Random **sampling** helps a sample represent a population, so results can generalize to that population. Random **assignment** balances groups in an experiment, so a difference can support a cause-and-effect conclusion for the studied participants. These are different kinds of randomness.

## Match the claim to the design

An observational study measures existing groups without assigning treatments. It can show an association, but other differences may explain it. A convenience sample may not represent the full population. A large sample does not fix a biased sampling method.

## Common traps

- Mixing up random selection with random assignment.
- Saying an association proves causation.
- Generalizing beyond the population the sample can represent.

## Can Desmos help?

Desmos can graph study results or compare group averages, but a graph cannot tell whether participants were randomly assigned or whether the sample was representative. Read the study description first; use the calculator only to check numerical summaries.

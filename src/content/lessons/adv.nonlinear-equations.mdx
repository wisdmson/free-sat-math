## Quadratics and their roots

The solutions to an equation are the input values that make it true. If a quadratic is factored, use the zero-product rule: when <M t="AB=0" />, at least one factor is zero. If it does not factor cleanly, use the quadratic formula or complete the square.

## Intersections and restrictions

For a system containing a line and a parabola, substitute the line rule into the quadratic. The solutions are the x-coordinates of their intersections. When squaring both sides or clearing a denominator, check every candidate in the original equation; those steps can introduce values that do not work.

## Common traps

- Stopping after finding one root of a factored quadratic.
- Forgetting the plus-or-minus in a square-root step.
- Keeping an extraneous solution without checking it in the original equation.

## Desmos steps

1. Enter the left and right sides as separate equations, for example `y=(x-2)(x+3)` and `y=0`.
2. Click each intersection to read its coordinates; the x-values solve the equation.
3. Substitute those values into the original equation, especially if you squared or multiplied by a variable expression.

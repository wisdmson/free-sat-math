## Pythagorean theorem

For a right triangle, <M t="a^2+b^2=c^2" />, where <M t="c" /> is always the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle. Special triples such as 3-4-5 and 5-12-13 can make calculations quick.

## Trigonometric ratios

Relative to an acute angle, use <M t="\sin\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{hypotenuse}" />, <M t="\cos\theta=\text{adjacent}/\text{hypotenuse}" />, or <M t="\tan\theta=\text{opposite}/\text{adjacent}" />. Label the sides from the angle named in the question before choosing a ratio.

## Common traps

- Calling a leg the hypotenuse; it is always opposite the right angle.
- Switching opposite and adjacent when the reference angle changes.
- Forgetting that the calculator must be in degree mode when angles are in degrees.

## Desmos steps

1. Check that the angle setting is degrees if the problem uses degree measures.
2. Enter a ratio such as `sin(30 degrees)=x/10` and solve for the missing side.
3. Use `a^2+b^2=c^2` for a right triangle, then check that the result is a positive length.
